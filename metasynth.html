How this works

This is a browser-based homage to MetaSynth's Image Synth room. You paint directly on a spectrogram: the horizontal axis is time, the vertical axis is pitch (log-scaled, like the human ear), and pixel color encodes loudness and stereo position:

  • Red channel → volume in the left speaker at that pitch/time.
  • Green channel → volume in the right speaker.
  • Blue channel → volume added equally to both speakers (center).
  • Brightness overall → how loud that partial is.

When you hit Render Audio, every row of pixels becomes a sine-wave oscillator tuned to that row's frequency. Its amplitude over time is read straight out of the pixels in that row (interpolated smoothly between columns), then every oscillator is summed together — this is additive synthesis, the same principle MetaSynth's inverse-FFT resynthesis is built on. Draw fine dots for clean tones, thick smears for noisy textures, diagonal lines for pitch sweeps, or load any photo to hear what it "sounds" like.

The Effects & Export room runs the rendered audio through a filter, delay, and reverb chain you can preview live and then bake into a downloadable WAV file.

Runs entirely client-side — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
